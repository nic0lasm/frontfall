# Game rules

Status: v1, every rule implemented · Owner: maintainers · Balance values: [balance.md](balance.md)

This document is the single source of truth for how Frontfall plays. It describes what a player sees and can do, not how the code does it. Code, tests and pull requests refer to rules by identifier, for example `R-ATTACK-03`. A change to the game starts with a change to this file.

Numbers are never written here. Each tunable value has a key such as `B-GROWTH-BASE`, defined in [balance.md](balance.md), so balancing a game never edits the rules.

Words used throughout:

| Term        | Meaning                                                                  |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Tile        | Smallest square of the map. Every rule about space counts in tiles.      |
| Tick        | One step of the simulation. There are `B-TICK-RATE` ticks per second.    |
| Territory   | The set of tiles a player owns.                                          |
| Border tile | An owned tile with at least one neighbour the player does not own.       |
| Troops      | The single resource that grows, attacks and defends.                     |
| Gold        | Currency spent on structures and units.                                  |
| Neighbour   | One of the four tiles sharing an edge, across the east-west seam if any. |

## 1. Map

- **R-MAP-01** The map is a grid of tiles. Each tile is land or water, and land has a relief level: plains, hills or mountains.
- **R-MAP-02** Some land is impassable: no player can own it and nothing can cross it. Ice caps near the poles are impassable by default.
- **R-MAP-03** A map either has edges on all four sides (flat) or wraps east to west (cylinder): moving east from the last column leads to the first one. North and south are always edges.
- **R-MAP-04** Distances and neighbours always take the shorter way around a wrapped map. No rule may treat the seam differently from any other column.
- **R-MAP-05** Water is either ocean (connected to the map's largest body of water) or a lake. Ships can only use water connected to the port they come from.
- **R-MAP-06** Relief slows conquest: capturing hills and mountains costs more troops and time than plains (`B-TILE-WEIGHT`).

## 2. Joining and spawning

- **R-SPAWN-01** A game starts with a spawn phase lasting `B-SPAWN-PHASE`. During it, each player picks a land tile to start from. Picking again moves the start.
- **R-SPAWN-02** A start tile must be unowned land (or land the player already holds from a previous pick), not impassable, and at least `B-SPAWN-DISTANCE` tiles from every other start.
- **R-SPAWN-03** A player who has not picked when the phase ends is placed on a random valid tile, drawn from the game's seed. If no tile is far enough from the others, the spacing of `R-SPAWN-02` is dropped; if no land is free at all, the player is not placed and is eliminated when play starts (`R-ECO-05`).
- **R-SPAWN-04** Each player starts with the tiles within `B-SPAWN-RADIUS` of their start tile, and `B-START-TROOPS` troops.
- **R-SPAWN-05** Nobody can attack or build during the spawn phase.
- **R-SPAWN-06** Spawn values fit every map size: `B-SPAWN-DISTANCE` and `B-SPAWN-RADIUS` grow with the square root of the map's area beyond 2^19 tiles, and `B-START-TROOPS` with the start area.

## 3. Economy

- **R-ECO-01** Each player has a population cap: a base everyone has (`B-POP-BASE`), so a player who has just spawned, or is down to a few tiles, still grows; their land, each tile counting for less as it grows (`B-POP-LAND`), so a large empire holds far fewer troops a tile than a small one; and a fixed amount for each working city (`R-BUILD-01`).
- **R-ECO-02** Troops grow every tick by `B-GROWTH-BASE`, plus a power of the army's strength over `B-GROWTH-DIVISOR`, times the share of the cap still free, and not at all at the cap: an empty army grows slowly, a larger one faster, until the cap slows it, so growth peaks a little under half the cap. Below the cap, a player always gains at least one troop per tick, so the cap is reached. Troops above the cap, back from an attack or left over after land is lost, do not grow: each second, `B-DESERTION` of the excess leaves, so an army cannot be stacked past its cap by sending troops out and calling them back.
- **R-ECO-03** A player earns gold every tick: a fixed income that does not depend on their size (`B-GOLD-BASE`), more for the share of their cap at work (`R-ECO-07`), and, in a game that sets it, a little for each troop they hold back from attacks (`B-GOLD-RATE`). Trade adds to this income (`R-UNIT-03`).
- **R-ECO-04** Troops and gold are whole numbers. When a computation produces a fraction, it is rounded down.
- **R-ECO-05** A player whose territory drops to zero tiles is eliminated. Their troops are lost; their gold goes to whoever eliminated them (`R-ECO-08`), or is lost.
- **R-ECO-06** Withdrawn: the land counting for less as it grows (`R-ECO-01`) does what the falloff past a share of the map did.
- **R-ECO-07** Each player chooses what share of their troops at home work, from 0 to `B-WORK-MAX`; it starts at 0. Workers earn up to `B-WORK-GOLD` more of the fixed income (`R-ECO-03`), in proportion to the share of the population cap they fill, never past the whole cap. Workers stay at home: an attack, a ship or a gift takes soldiers only, and only soldiers count towards the price of taking the player's land (`R-ATTACK-03`). Workers grow and die like any troops: the share applies to whatever is at home, and a tile lost costs its share of all the troops on it.
- **R-ECO-08** The player who takes the last tile of another, by an attack, a landing, an encirclement or their capital (`R-CAP-03`), eliminates them and takes all of their gold, bots and nations alike. Land that goes back to nobody, as under a missile (`R-UNIT-05`), eliminates nobody, and the gold is lost. Every player is told, and the one who took the gold sees it added.

## 4. Expansion and attacks

- **R-ATTACK-01** A player expands by sending troops from their territory towards a target: unowned land or a neighbouring player. The target can be any tile of that owner, however far: the attack pushes along the whole shared border and heads for that tile. A player has at most one attack against each owner; a new order against the same owner adds its troops to the running attack and moves its aim.
- **R-ATTACK-02** The attack ratio, set by the player between `B-ATTACK-RATIO-MIN` and 100 %, decides the share of current soldiers (`R-ECO-07`) each new attack takes.
- **R-ATTACK-03** An attack advances along the shared border, one tile at a time, taking first the tiles closest in a straight line to the point the player aimed at; a tile that shares sides with the attacker's land counts as `B-ATTACK-SPREAD` tiles closer for each, so the front widens into a wedge. On unowned land, larger attacks take more tiles per tick (`B-ATTACK-SPEED`). Against a player, an attack moves at the pace of its front: each tick it takes up to `B-ATTACK-PACE` of the number of the defender's tiles touching its land, when its troops reach `B-ATTACK-PACE-ODDS` times the garrison facing it (the defender's soldiers per tile times those tiles), and less in proportion when they fall short, at least one tile; `B-ATTACK-SPEED` still bounds it. Taking a player's land therefore takes time, and both sides lose troops all along. Each captured tile costs the attacker troops, in units of its weight, more for relief (`B-TILE-WEIGHT`, `R-MAP-06`): the weight times the odds the attack faces, the defender's soldiers per troop of the attack held between `B-ODDS-MIN` and `B-ODDS-MAX`, times `B-LOSS-BASE` plus `B-LOSS-DENSITY` for each soldier of the defender per tile. A huge territory pays less on either side: the base falls by up to `B-LARGE-ATTACKER` for the attacker's size and `B-LARGE-DEFENDER` for the defender's, half of it at `B-LARGE-MID` tiles. A bot's land costs players and nations `B-BOT-DEFENSE` of its price. Prices are worked out in fractions of a troop; a fraction rounds up on that share of tiles, picked from the tile alone. The defender loses their average troops per tile with each tile taken. Every attack's front for a tick is chosen before any attack of that tick moves.
- **R-ATTACK-04** Unowned land costs troops to take but never fights back: `B-NEUTRAL-SHARE` of the tile's weight, `B-BOT-NEUTRAL-SHARE` for a bot.
- **R-ATTACK-05** An attack ends when its troops run out, when no reachable target tile remains, or when its player retreats.
- **R-ATTACK-06** Retreating returns the attack's remaining troops to the player, minus a penalty (`B-RETREAT-PENALTY`).
- **R-ATTACK-07** Two attacks between the same players in opposite directions cancel out first: troops fight troops before any tile changes hands.
- **R-ATTACK-08** Territory that ends up surrounded by one other player, with no border to any third party or to unowned land, is captured by that player at once, whether or not the attack was aimed at its owner. Water and impassable land count as part of the surrounding; pockets larger than `B-ENCIRCLE-MAX` tiles are not captured this way, nor is all the land its owner has left, which must be fought for, nor a pocket next to the aim of an attack its owner has under way against the surrounding player, such as a beachhead its troops are fighting from.
- **R-ATTACK-09** Players can attack across water only with ships (`R-UNIT-02`).
- **R-ATTACK-10** Collapsing fronts, a special rule (`R-MODE-08`). On each tick, an attack on a player is overwhelming when its troops are at least `B-COLLAPSE-ODDS` times the garrison facing it: the defender's soldiers per tile (`R-ECO-07`) times the number of their tiles that touch the attacker's land. An attack that takes land while overwhelming builds pressure, and loses it all on any tick it is not or takes nothing. Once it has built up `B-COLLAPSE-BUILDUP`, the front gives way: from the first tile it took that tick, the one its front ranked closest to its aim (`R-ATTACK-03`) and still next to the defender, the defender's land joined to that tile and within `B-COLLAPSE-DEPTH` of it falls at once, nearest first, for `B-COLLAPSE-COST` of its usual price, as long as the attack can pay; the defender loses their troops per tile for each tile, as in `R-ATTACK-03`. Land within reach of one of the defender's ready forts (`R-BUILD-03`) never gives way. Land that the breach cuts off then falls by encirclement (`R-ATTACK-08`), and the pressure starts again from nothing. Both players are told when a front gives way. A thin front held by few soldiers, with workers at home, breaks first; sending troops back to arms, opposing the attack (`R-ATTACK-07`) or a fort stops it.

## 5. Diplomacy

Diplomacy starts with the play phase, like attacks.

- **R-DIPLO-01** A player can ask any other player still in the game for an alliance. It is formed when the asked player asks back; the request expires after `B-ALLIANCE-REQUEST-TIMEOUT` if not. One request at a time waits between two players, and after one expires its sender waits `B-ALLIANCE-REQUEST-COOLDOWN` before asking the same player again.
- **R-DIPLO-02** Allies cannot attack each other, by land or by sea, and their units do not fire at each other. When an alliance is formed, the attacks between the two players stop and their troops go home as from a retreat (`R-ATTACK-06`); a ship that reaches an ally's coast brings its troops home too. Encirclement (`R-ATTACK-08`) never takes an ally's land.
- **R-DIPLO-03** An alliance lasts `B-ALLIANCE-DURATION`, then ends with no penalty. In its last `B-ALLIANCE-RENEWAL`, each ally may ask to renew it; once both have, it starts again for a full `B-ALLIANCE-DURATION`.
- **R-DIPLO-04** To attack an ally, a player must first betray them, which ends the alliance at once. The betrayer is marked a traitor for `B-TRAITOR-DURATION`: their land costs `B-TRAITOR-DEFENSE` of the usual price to take, and every player is told. The betrayer then waits `B-ALLIANCE-REQUEST-COOLDOWN` before asking the betrayed player again.
- **R-DIPLO-05** A player can place an embargo on any player but a teammate, at no cost and at once, and lift it the same way. While either of two players holds an embargo on the other, no trade ship sails between them, and those at sea between them are lost on arrival. Every player is told when an embargo starts or is lifted; an embargo also ends, unannounced, when either player falls.
- **R-DIPLO-06** A player can send an ally or a teammate troops, gold or both, at once. The receiver takes only the troops their population cap has room for; the rest stay with the sender. Gifts are refused between players at war (`R-UNIT-04`) and to anyone who is not an ally or a teammate, and every player is told who sent a gift to whom. A gift is the sender's own choice, even when it keeps troops out of a missile's reach or pays for someone else's silo. Bots send none; nations send troops to an ally in need (`R-BOT-24`). Both take what they are given.
- **R-DIPLO-10** A player can pay tribute to a player who holds at least `B-TRIBUTE-LEAD` of their land: they pay `B-TRIBUTE-BASE` plus `B-TRIBUTE-PER-TILE` for each tile they hold, at once, to that player, and the two are in a truce for `B-TRIBUTE-DURATION`. A truce stops attacks, landings and fire between the two like an alliance (`R-DIPLO-02`), but is no alliance: it takes no gift, counts for no nation's allies, cannot be renewed, and no alliance can be asked while it runs. Either of them may break it with `betray` and is marked a traitor (`R-DIPLO-04`); otherwise it runs out with no penalty, and the weaker player may pay again. A teammate or an ally takes none. Every player is told.
- **R-DIPLO-07** A player can send any other player still in the game one of a fixed list of quick messages and emoji, which interfaces show to the two of them only, one message every 2 seconds at most. Free text chat is not part of v1. Messages change nothing else in the game. Bots neither send nor answer them; nations send some and heed a few (`R-BOT-20`, `R-BOT-21`).
- **R-DIPLO-08** A player can hide another player, on their own screen only. A hidden player's name is replaced by a number everywhere, on the map, in the leaderboard, the player menu and the events, and their quick messages are no longer shown. Hiding changes nothing in the game, and the player can show them again at any time.
- **R-DIPLO-09** A player who attacks or lands troops on another player's land finds that player's ports shut to them: the victim places an embargo on the attacker (`R-DIPLO-05`) that lasts `B-EMBARGO-AUTO`, unless one already stands between them. Every player is told. It ends early when the two become allies. The victim may keep it for good by placing the embargo themselves, or lift it like any other. It cuts trade only: it neither puts the two at war nor lets warships take trade ships (`R-UNIT-04`).

## 6. Structures

Every structure is built on an owned land tile, costs gold, paid when the order is carried out, and takes `B-BUILD-TIME` before it works, `B-BUILD-TIME-LONG` for a missile silo or an air defense. Costs rise with the number of the same structure already owned, those still being built included (`B-COST-GROWTH`). A structure captured with its tile changes owner, finished or not.

- **R-BUILD-01** City: raises the population cap by `B-POP-PER-CITY` once ready (`R-ECO-01`).
- **R-BUILD-02** Port (`B-COST-PORT`): built on a coastal tile of the player's land, lake shores included; a port on a lake trades only with ports on the same lake. Once built it sends trade ships (`R-UNIT-03`). Transport ships do not need one: they leave from any coast (`R-UNIT-02`).
- **R-BUILD-03** Fort: on the owner's tiles within `B-FORT-RADIUS`, attackers pay `B-FORT-STRENGTH` of the usual price per tile, and the defender loses `B-FORT-LOSSES` of the usual troops per tile lost. Land taken by encirclement (`R-ATTACK-08`) pays no price, fort or not: a fortified pocket smaller than `B-ENCIRCLE-MAX` falls whole once surrounded, which on small maps can be all the land a fort covers.
- **R-BUILD-04** Missile silo (`B-COST-SILO`): holds one missile, launched by the player at any land tile (`R-UNIT-05`), after which it reloads for `B-SILO-RELOAD`. A launch leaves from the player's loaded silo closest to the target.
- **R-BUILD-05** Air defense (`B-COST-AIR-DEFENSE`): once per missile, on the first tick a missile of a player who is not its owner's ally flies within `B-AIR-DEFENSE-RADIUS` of it while the air defense is loaded, it tries to stop it and does so at `B-AIR-DEFENSE-CHANCE`, drawn from the game's seed. One that stops a missile tries again only after `B-AIR-DEFENSE-RELOAD`.
- **R-BUILD-06** A structure can be demolished by its owner, with no refund.
- **R-BUILD-07** Two structures, whoever owns them, stand at least `B-BUILD-SPACING` tiles apart. A player may own as many cities as they can pay for, each dearer than the last (`B-COST-GROWTH`, up to `B-COST-MAX`). Ports grow in price the same way; forts and air defenses cost one base price more for each one owned, up to `B-COST-FORT-MAX` and `B-COST-AIR-DEFENSE-MAX`; silos always cost the same. A game can limit them to one, and one more for every `B-CITY-LAND` tiles of land (none by default); cities beyond what their land supports, after a loss of land or a capture, then do not count towards the cap.
- **R-BUILD-08** Micho Palace: two cities on one tile. It costs what the player's next two cities would, counts as two cities towards the land limit when there is one (`R-BUILD-07`) and the cost of later cities and palaces, and once ready raises the population cap as two cities do (`R-BUILD-01`). It is built, captured and demolished like a city, and a game that leaves out cities leaves out palaces too (`R-MODE-03`).
- **R-BUILD-09** Mosque of Cyrus: an air defense worth two. It costs what the player's next two air defenses would and counts as two towards the cost of later air defenses and mosques. It stops missiles as `R-BUILD-05` does, with the same radius, chance and reload, but gets two independent tries at each missile, so it stops one far more often than a single air defense (94 % against 75 % at the default chance). It is built, captured and demolished like an air defense, and a game that leaves out air defenses leaves out mosques too (`R-MODE-03`). Nations do not build it.

## 7. Units

- **R-UNIT-01** Units move over the map, never through impassable terrain, and are visible to every player.
- **R-UNIT-02** Transport ship: carries troops by sea to a coastal tile the player does not own, and lands them there (`R-ATTACK-09`).
  - It leaves from the player's coast closest to the target by sea (from a port, once ports exist), takes the shortest sea route and moves `B-BOAT-SPEED` water tiles per tick. Routes longer than `B-BOAT-RANGE` are refused, and a player has at most `B-BOAT-MAX` ships at sea.
  - On arrival it takes the landing tile at the price an attack would pay, and the remaining troops attack the tile's former owner from there, by the rules of `R-ATTACK`. If the player already owns the landing tile, the troops simply rejoin them; if they cannot pay for it, they are lost.
  - It sinks with its troops if its player is eliminated, or if it is destroyed at sea (`R-UNIT-04`).
- **R-UNIT-03** Trade ship: every `B-TRADE-INTERVAL` from the moment it was built, a port with fewer than `B-TRADE-MAX` of its ships at sea rolls for a ship, drawn from the game's seed. The roll succeeds one time in `B-TRADE-ODDS` for a player with many ports, more often for fewer ports, more often after each miss, and less often the more trade ships sail the seas. On a success, a ship sails at `B-BOAT-SPEED` to a port of another player within `B-TRADE-RANGE` of sea, drawn among those not cut off by an embargo (`R-DIPLO-05`), far ports and allies' ports twice as likely; allies and teammates trade like anyone else. If a port of another player still stands at the destination when it arrives, and no embargo has come between them, its owner and the destination's owner each earn gold that grows with the length of the route, most steeply around a medium length (see balance.md). Otherwise the ship is lost. A fallen player's trade ships sink.
- **R-UNIT-04** Warship: bought at one of the player's ready ports (`B-COST-WARSHIP`, one base price dearer for each one owned up to `B-COST-MAX`, at most `B-WARSHIP-MAX`), it starts on the port's water and patrols within `B-WARSHIP-PATROL` of its patrol tile, which the player can move to any water it can reach. Its port being taken or destroyed does not stop it; it sinks if its player is eliminated.
  - Two players are at war while an attack, a transport ship or a missile of either against the other is under way or ended less than `B-WAR-MEMORY` ago, or while an embargo stands between them (`R-DIPLO-05`). Allies and teammates never are.
  - Every `B-WARSHIP-RELOAD`, a warship fires at the closest ship within `B-WARSHIP-RANGE` of a player at war with its owner, warships before transport ships. Warships fire in the order they were bought, and one sunk earlier in the tick no longer fires. A warship hit loses `B-WARSHIP-DAMAGE` of its `B-WARSHIP-HEALTH` and sinks at none; a transport ship sinks at the first hit, with its troops.
  - Instead of a shot, it takes the oldest trade ship within `B-WARSHIP-RANGE` of a player, not an ally, with whom an embargo has stood for at least `B-EMBARGO-NOTICE`, whichever of the two placed it: an embargo breaks trade both ways, and either side's warships take the other's ships. Its owner earns what the ship would have paid each side on arrival, once; the port the ship sailed for earns nothing. Every player is told.
- **R-UNIT-05** Missile (`B-COST-MISSILE`, or `B-COST-HEAVY-MISSILE` for a heavy one, the same price every time): flies from a silo (`R-BUILD-04`) to its target in a straight line, the short way around a wrapping map, at `B-MISSILE-SPEED`. A launch is refused when its blast would reach land of an ally or a teammate; a player may target their own land. Air defenses may stop it on the way (`R-BUILD-05`). If land under its blast belongs to an ally or a teammate when it arrives, it falls harmlessly and its price is paid back.
  - Where it strikes, every tile of land within its blast radius (`B-MISSILE-RADIUS`, `B-HEAVY-MISSILE-RADIUS`) goes back to nobody, with the structures on it, whoever held it, the launcher included. Each player hit loses `B-MISSILE-TROOPS` of the share of their troops that those tiles were of their land, never more than all of them: soldiers stand thickest where a missile is aimed.
  - Every player is told of a launch at another player's land, and of a missile stopped.
- **R-UNIT-06** Fallout: a missile leaves the land within its blast radius irradiated, for `B-FALLOUT-TIME-LIGHT` under a light one and `B-FALLOUT-TIME` under a heavy one. Taking a tile under fallout, by attack or landing, costs `B-FALLOUT-COST` of its usual price; when the fallout clears, it is unowned land like any other. The blast is a disc of the blast radius, measured in a straight line, not along the grid.

## 8. Bots and nations

- **R-BOT-01** Bots are simple players that fill empty maps: they expand into unowned land and attack weak neighbours. They never ask for an alliance and answer requests by `R-BOT-10`.
- **R-BOT-02** Nations are stronger computer players named after the countries of a map, each starting from the tile the map gives it. They follow the bot rules above, build, and retaliate when attacked (`R-BOT-07` to `R-BOT-09`). They remember what other players do to them, say so with quick messages, ask for alliances and answer them, help their allies and may betray them (`R-BOT-19` to `R-BOT-25`).
- **R-BOT-03** Computer players follow the same rules as humans and send their orders the same way. They take decisions only from the game state and the game's seed, so every client computes the same decisions.
- **R-BOT-04** The difficulty setting changes how often and how boldly computer players act, and how many troops nations raise (`R-BOT-26`), never the rules they follow. A game starts at easy unless its player, or its host online, picks another; public games are always at easy.
- **R-BOT-05** A bot with nothing worth taking by land sends a transport ship to the closest unowned coast it can reach away from its own shore, or else to the coast of a weaker player there, with up to `B-BOAT-MAX` ships at sea. A large empire looks at more of its border before deciding, so a narrow land front is not missed.
- **R-BOT-06** A bot whose gold pays for a city its land supports builds one on the free inland tile closest to its start, or to one of its border tiles once the start is lost. Once it has a city, it builds one port, on its free tile by the ocean (`R-MAP-05`) closest to its start within `B-BOT-PORT-SEARCH`, when its gold allows and while it still holds its start. Bots never place an embargo.
- **R-BOT-07** When the spawn phase ends, before players who did not pick are placed (`R-SPAWN-03`), each nation starts on its tile if that tile is still unowned, without the spacing of `R-SPAWN-02`. A player whose start area covers it took the nation's place: the nation does not play.
- **R-BOT-08** A nation answers the player whose attacks on its land hold the most troops, and only a player, not a bot or a city-state: at hard at once, before anything else, sending at least what comes at it (`B-NATION-ANSWER`); at normal once its troops are above its reserve (`R-BOT-26`); at easy as the first of its strategies (`R-BOT-27`). When its gold allows, it builds a fort behind its border with that player, unless one of its forts already covers it. While that player holds no land along its border, as when its ships have not landed yet, it cannot answer by land.
- **R-BOT-09** A nation chooses its targets as `R-BOT-26` and `R-BOT-27` say. Unowned land is always open to it. It decides every `B-NATION-INTERVAL`, in place of the bot value, and otherwise sails and builds cities and a port like a bot (`R-BOT-05`, `R-BOT-06`). No grace protects players at the start (`B-NATION-GRACE` is 0 since 0.9.5): the trigger share and `B-NATION-HUMAN` pace nations instead.
- **R-BOT-10** A bot accepts an alliance from a player with less land than itself, not marked a traitor, with no attack between them, while the two of them hold less than `B-WIN-SHARE` of the land together and the bot has fewer than `B-BOT-ALLIANCE-MAX` allies, and renews an alliance on the same terms. In a team game, each side's land is its whole team's, and teammates count among the bot's allies. A bot never betrays, and never attacks or sails to an ally.
- **R-BOT-11** While a transport ship of a player who is not its ally sails for its coast, a nation buys a warship at its oldest ready port, as long as it has fewer warships than ports and than `B-NATION-WARSHIPS`, when its gold allows. Bots buy none.
- **R-BOT-12** A nation with `B-NATION-SILO-CITIES` cities, whose gold pays for a silo and a missile, builds one silo on its free inland tile closest to its start, while it holds its start. Once a player who is not its ally owns a silo, it builds one air defense the same way when its gold allows. Whenever a silo of its is loaded and its gold pays for a missile, it launches one at the city closest to its start of the player attacking it (`R-BOT-08`), or else of the other player with the most land, once that player holds `B-NATION-MISSILE-LEADER` of the land. After a launch it waits `B-NATION-MISSILE-PAUSE` before the next one. It never launches a heavy missile. Bots build neither.
- **R-BOT-13** Retired in 0.9.5: nations no longer hold back from a player that others are already attacking; how often they let a player be is `B-NATION-HUMAN` (`R-BOT-27`).
- **R-BOT-14** A nation that has the crown among its strategies (`R-BOT-27`) turns on the leader: the player, nation or not, whose land is above `B-NATION-RUNAWAY` of the runner-up's. It joins the attacks already on that leader, at normal only once they hold a tenth of the leader's troops, and calls on its allies to do the same (`R-BOT-21`). At a difficulty where `B-NATION-RUNAWAY` is 0, nations never do.
- **R-BOT-15** A nation whose troops are at `B-NATION-WORK-FROM` of its population cap, that fights no player and whom nobody holds (`R-BOT-08`), puts `B-NATION-WORK` of its troops at home to work (`R-ECO-07`). It puts them all back under arms as soon as someone holds it, its army is no longer full, or before it attacks a player, and weighs its troops (`R-BOT-26`) all together. Bots never put troops to work.
- **R-BOT-16** In a game with collapsing fronts (`R-ATTACK-10`), a nation with an attack on a player holding more land than itself that is building pressure sends it everything it can spare at each decision, after answering its own attacker (`R-BOT-08`) and before anything else. Bots do not.
- **R-BOT-17** In capital mode (`R-CAP`), every attack of a bot or a nation on a player heads for that player's capital (`R-ATTACK-01`). A nation whose capital is under threat (`R-CAP-05`) takes that attacker as the one it answers (`R-BOT-08`) and builds its first fort by its capital. A nation with the capital among its strategies (`R-BOT-27`) attacks the capital of a player who is not its ally, has fewer troops than it and whose capital lies within `B-CAPITAL-ALERT` of a sample of its border.
- **R-BOT-18** Now and then, one decision in `B-NATION-SHIP` (`B-NATION-SHIP-ALONE` without a land neighbour), a nation sends a ship (`R-BOT-05`) with a fifth of its soldiers, to free land or to a player it would turn on (`R-BOT-27`); at hard never while it is attacked, at normal one time in two. A nation that must strike a player without a land border with them does so by sea, with at most a fifth of its soldiers.
- **R-BOT-19** A nation answers each request for an alliance once, the same way each time it weighs it, and asks on the same terms (`R-BOT-28`). It takes the hand of a player the difficulty makes it fear (`B-NATION-THREAT-ALLY`), refuses a traitor nine times in ten, the leader it is turning on (`R-BOT-14`) and anyone it is wary of (`R-BOT-20`), takes a friend's hand but `B-NATION-FRIEND-REFUSE` of the time, and none past `B-NATION-ALLIES` allies, or one more. During its first `B-NATION-EARLY` of play it takes `B-NATION-EARLY-ACCEPT` of the other hands; later only from a player about as strong: troops above `B-NATION-SIMILAR-TROOPS` of its own, or land above `B-NATION-SIMILAR-LAND` with at least half its troops. One choice in `B-NATION-CONFUSED` is made at random. A nation never attacks, sails to or fires on an ally, until it betrays them (`R-BOT-25`). Nobody can ally with a city-state (`R-BOT-29`).
- **R-BOT-20** Each nation keeps a score of every other player, from -100 to 100, 0 at the start. It loses `B-NATION-MOOD-ATTACK` for each attack or landing of theirs on its land, down to -60, `B-NATION-MOOD-MISSILE` for a missile on its land, and `B-NATION-MOOD-ANGRY` for an angry face, down to -10; the nation they betray drops to -100, and every other nation loses `B-NATION-MOOD-TRAITOR`, down to -60. It gains `B-NATION-MOOD-GIFT` for a gift worth `B-NATION-GIFT-SHARE` of its troops or gold, `B-NATION-MOOD-ALLIED` when an alliance with them is formed or renewed, and `B-NATION-MOOD-HELP` for each attack of theirs on a player attacking it; an apology brings back `B-NATION-MOOD-SORRY`, up to 0. A bad score heals by a point every `B-NATION-MOOD-HEAL`, a good one fades by a point every `B-NATION-MOOD-FADE` while they are not allies. At or below `B-NATION-MOOD-HOSTILE` the nation is hostile to them, below `B-NATION-MOOD-WARY` wary, at or above `B-NATION-MOOD-FRIENDLY` friendly, and neutral otherwise; every player sees how each nation feels about them. A nation places an embargo on players it is hostile to, which it lifts once it is no longer wary of them.
- **R-BOT-21** A nation sends quick messages (`R-DIPLO-07`) to players who are not nations, one at a time: hello to a new neighbour and to a greeting, once a game; leave me alone when it turns wary of them and an angry face when it turns hostile; thanks for a gift that counts; a thumbs up when an alliance is formed, a thumbs down to a request it turns down; and, when it turns on the leader (`R-BOT-14`), a call on its allies to do the same, at most every 2 minutes. It heeds help me and let's be allies for 60 s (`R-BOT-23`, `R-BOT-24`).
- **R-BOT-22** A nation with the traitor among its strategies (`R-BOT-27`) turns on a neighbour who betrayed anyone, when that neighbour has fewer than 1.2 times its troops, by land or else by sea. At a difficulty with `B-NATION-GRUDGE`, its grudge comes before that: it turns on the player it is hostile to (`R-BOT-20`).
- **R-BOT-23** Every `B-NATION-COURT`, a nation greets its new neighbours, places and lifts embargoes (`R-BOT-20`) and eyes its allies (`R-BOT-25`). Its own requests for alliances follow `R-BOT-28`.
- **R-BOT-24** A nation with assistance among its strategies (`R-BOT-27`) answers an attack on an ally it likes or who asked for help: it attacks the player attacking that ally the most, if they border it. Otherwise, at most once every `B-NATION-GIFT-PAUSE`, it gives `B-NATION-GIFT` of its soldiers, while it has more than twice its reserve, to an ally who asked for help or who is under attack with fewer than `B-NATION-NEED` of their cap.
- **R-BOT-25** Settled, at peace and with no attack on it, a nation not marked a traitor eyes an ally that borders it with at most `B-NATION-BETRAY-LAND` of its land and `B-NATION-BETRAY-TROOPS` of its troops. Its score of that ally drops by `B-NATION-COVET` every `B-NATION-COURT`, which every player sees; it sends a thumbs down when it turns wary, and at `B-NATION-BETRAY-SCORE` it says sorry, betrays (`R-DIPLO-04`) and turns on them for 2 minutes as on a traitor (`R-BOT-22`). An ally who grows, gathers troops or is liked enough is spared.
- **R-BOT-26** Each nation draws three shares of its population cap from its number: `B-NATION-TRIGGER`, `B-NATION-KEEP` and `B-NATION-EXPAND`. It takes free land first, sending what lies above `B-NATION-EXPAND`. It saves up to `B-NATION-KEEP`, then turns on a player only once its troops reach `B-NATION-TRIGGER`, or at one decision in ten whatever its troops, sending what lies above `B-NATION-KEEP`. The difficulty sets a nation's population cap (`B-NATION-CAP`), starting troops (`B-NATION-START`) and growth (`B-NATION-GROWTH`) against a player's.
- **R-BOT-27** Once past its trigger, a nation tries its strategies in the order its difficulty gives (`B-NATION-STRATEGIES`), and attacks with the first that finds a target: answering its attacker (`R-BOT-08`), helping an ally (`R-BOT-24`), a grudge or a traitor (`R-BOT-22`), the crown (`R-BOT-14`), a capital within reach (`R-BOT-17`), a very weak neighbour (under 15 % of their cap), the juiciest neighbour (buildings, troops and land against the cost), the victim of others' attacks, its weakest neighbour with fewer troops than its own, or a landing when it has no land neighbour. It goes on against a nation, a city-state or a traitor every time, against any other player `B-NATION-HUMAN` of the time. At hard it keeps home `B-NATION-RETAIN` of its strongest other neighbour's troops, and sends nothing that would be under `B-NATION-TOO-WEAK` of the target's troops unless it is attacked.
- **R-BOT-28** At each decision, a nation asks each of its neighbours for an alliance one time in `B-NATION-ASK`, on its own terms (`R-BOT-19`), and answers the requests it has.
- **R-BOT-29** City-states are small computer players that come with the nations, `B-CITYSTATE-PER-NATION` for each, placed at random when the spawn phase ends: the land and the gold that every player, above all early on, takes from them. They hold `B-CITYSTATE-CAP` of a player's population cap, grow at `B-CITYSTATE-GROWTH`, start with `B-CITYSTATE-START` of the troops and earn `B-CITYSTATE-GOLD` of the base gold; their land is priced as a bot's (`R-ATTACK-04`), and their gold goes to whoever takes their last tile (`R-ECO-08`). Every `B-CITYSTATE-INTERVAL`, a city-state may turn on a traitor beside it, takes free land, and once past its trigger answers whoever attacks it or attacks a neighbour drawn at random. It takes no allies, and nobody can ally with it; it builds nothing, has no moods and sends no messages. Interfaces do not tell of a city-state's fall unless the player took it.

## 9. Victory and game modes

- **R-WIN-01** A player wins by owning `B-WIN-SHARE` of all ownable land, or by being the last player standing.
- **R-WIN-02** A game that reaches `B-MAX-DURATION` ends; the player with the most territory wins, the lowest player number on a tie.
- **R-WIN-03** Once a game is decided it goes on. Eliminated players can keep watching it; a player still standing keeps playing.
- **R-WIN-04** In capital mode (`R-CAP`), a player also falls when another takes their capital, and the one who took it gets their empire: the last player standing wins sooner.
- **R-MODE-01** Free-for-all: every player for themselves; alliances follow `R-DIPLO`.
- **R-MODE-02** Teams: 2, 3 or 4 teams. Every player, bots and nations included, is dealt into a team in turn by player number, so team sizes differ by at most one. Teammates are allies for the whole game: they cannot attack each other, ask each other for an alliance or betray each other. A team wins when its members together own `B-WIN-SHARE` of the ownable land, or when it is the last team standing; at `B-MAX-DURATION`, the team with the most land wins. The team's member with the most land is named the winner.
- **R-MODE-03** Lobbies, public or private, let their host choose the map, the mode, bots and nations, and leave any of cities (palaces with them), forts, ports, missile silos, air defenses and warships out of the game. Leaving out ports also leaves out trade ships and warships, as they need one, and embargoes, with no trade left to cut; leaving out silos leaves out missiles. No one, bots and nations included, can build or buy what the game leaves out.
- **R-MODE-04** Every online game has a host, its first player, who sets its options and starts it. A public game needs at least two players to start; a private one can start with its host alone. The host can have the game start as soon as its seats are taken. A game opened by "Play now", which only ever sends players to such games, and only while their host has kept the usual options, also starts by itself 60 seconds after its first player arrived, and no sooner than 30 seconds after the second one did, with bots standing in for opponents if that player is still alone; its host may stop that countdown once. A host who leaves, or stays away for two minutes while others wait, hands the role to another player.
- **R-MODE-05** Before the start, the host can remove any other player from the lobby. That player is told, and cannot join that game again from the same network. A public game takes at most three players from one network, so that one person cannot fill it with their own tabs; a private game, shared by invitation, has no such limit.
- **R-MODE-06** In an online game, any player can report another person of the same game to the operator of the server, picking one reason from a short list; nobody else is told, the reported player included. The operator can ban a network from the server, for a while or for good, without restarting it: a banned network cannot connect, and its open connections close at once. Reports are forgotten after 30 days. Bots and nations cannot be reported.
- **R-MODE-07** A game runs at half, one, two or four times its normal pace. Solo, the player can switch between them at any time, as they can pause and resume. Online, the host of a private game picks one in the lobby, for the whole game; a public game always runs at the normal pace. The pace changes only how many ticks pass in a second, never what a tick does: every duration of the rules and of the balance is counted in ticks, so at twice the pace everything happens twice as soon, and at half the pace twice as late, the time limit (`B-MAX-DURATION`) included, and a game's record replays the same whatever pace it was played at. The normal pace itself runs `B-BASE-PACE` of the simulation's nominal rate, so that a game at speed 1 closes three turns every four beats of the clock.
- **R-MODE-08** Special rules are rules of Frontfall's own that a game is played with or without: collapsing fronts (`R-ATTACK-10`), capital mode (`R-CAP`) and doctrines (`R-DOCTRINE`). The host of a private game, or the player of a solo game, turns each one on or off before the start, in a menu of its own apart from the other options; public games and quick play use the defaults of the balance pass (`B-SPECIAL-RULES`). The lobby, the end screen and the game's record say which ones a game was played with, and none of them changes during a game.
- **R-MODE-09** Operator's test bonuses. The operator of a server, signed in to an account the server lists as theirs, may give themselves any of four bonuses in a private game they host, to test the game faster: twice the gold they earn (`R-ECO-03`, `R-ECO-07`), twice their population cap and troop growth (`R-ECO-01`, `R-ECO-02`), structures at half their price (`R-BUILD`), and land that costs twice the troops to take from them (`R-ATTACK-03`). They are set in the lobby before the start, for the whole game, and go to the operator alone, never to the other players. They do not exist in public games, quick play or solo, and no name, guest or other account can have them. The lobby and the end screen tell every player of that game who has which, and the game counts towards no statistics or achievements.

### Capital mode

A special rule (`R-MODE-08`), off by default, in private and solo games. Every player has a capital, and losing it costs the whole empire, so games are shorter and every front near a capital matters.

- **R-CAP-01** When the spawn phase ends, each player's start tile becomes their capital. Every player sees every capital.
- **R-CAP-02** Attackers pay `B-CAPITAL-STRENGTH` of the usual price for the owner's tiles within `B-CAPITAL-RADIUS` of the capital. Where a fort covers the same tile (`R-BUILD-03`), only the stronger of the two applies. Once capitals stand (`R-CAP-06`), the capital's own tile also costs `B-CAPITAL-GARRISON` of its owner's soldiers.
- **R-CAP-03** A capital falls when another player holds its tile, by an attack, a landing or an encirclement. At once, that player takes all of the fallen player's land, with the structures on it, `B-CAPITAL-SPOILS` of their troops, the rest being lost, and all of their gold, as they eliminate them (`R-ECO-08`); their troops in attacks and at sea are lost with them. Capitals that fall in the same tick are settled in player number order. Every player is told.
- **R-CAP-04** A capital whose tile goes back to nobody, as under a missile (`R-UNIT-05`), or to an ally or a teammate, is razed: its owner loses `B-CAPITAL-RAZE` of their troops, and the capital moves to the tile of theirs closest to it. Every player is told.
- **R-CAP-05** A capital is under threat while a player attacking its owner holds land within `B-CAPITAL-ALERT` of it. Interfaces warn its owner, and name the attacker.
- **R-CAP-06** Capitals stand only after `B-CAPITAL-GRACE` of play: before, a capital whose tile is taken simply moves to the tile its owner holds closest to it, at no cost, and no capital is under threat. Interfaces count down to the moment capitals stand.

### Doctrines

A special rule (`R-MODE-08`), off by default until the balance pass has measured it. Every player plays one of three doctrines, each a small edge bought with a small cost.

- **R-DOCTRINE-01** During the spawn phase, a player picks a doctrine, and may pick again until the phase ends. A player who has not picked plays the balanced doctrine, which changes nothing. Nobody can change doctrine once play starts.
- **R-DOCTRINE-02** The doctrines and their effects, as a change to the usual figure (`B-DOCTRINE-*`):
  - conqueror: attacks take land `B-DOCTRINE-PACE` faster (`R-ATTACK-03`) and lose fewer troops on a player's tile, but the fixed income (`R-ECO-03`) and the population cap (`R-ECO-01`) are lower;
  - merchant: trade ships pay `B-DOCTRINE-TRADE` more to their owner (`R-TRADE`) and the fixed income is higher, but attacks lose more troops on a player's tile;
  - bastion: the population cap is higher, a player loses fewer troops with each tile taken from them, and forts cost less (`R-BUILD`), but attacks are slower.
- **R-DOCTRINE-03** Choices stay private during the spawn phase; once play starts, every player sees every doctrine, on the leaderboard and in the end screen.
- **R-DOCTRINE-04** A nation picks its doctrine on the first tick of the spawn phase, from the game's random generator: easy nations draw evenly among the three; the others lean toward the merchant on a coast, the bastion when another nation is close and the conqueror otherwise, so that none is drawn less than a fifth or more than two fifths of the time. Bots and city-states play the balanced doctrine.
- **R-DOCTRINE-05** A doctrine changes figures of the rules only, never what a rule does: nothing in a doctrine skips a price, a cap or a cost for anyone else, and the victory share, the price of free land and the opening are the same for all.
- **R-DOCTRINE-06** The balance pass plays the same human-like player once per doctrine, in turn on every seat, over many games (`pnpm balance:doctrines`). No doctrine may hold more than 1.5 times its fair share of the land or of the victories, and none less than 0.6 times, over 1,000 games.

### Landmarks

Some maps, Paris and Lyon, have landmarks: famous places of the city, each with an effect of its own.

- **R-LAND-01** A map may list landmarks, each on a tile of land a player can own, with a name and one effect. Every player sees every landmark. A landmark belongs to whoever holds its tile, and changes hands with it, by attack, landing, encirclement or a missile; nobody holds it before.
- **R-LAND-02** While a player holds a landmark, they get its effect, and once more for each other landmark of the same effect they hold:
  - gold: `B-LANDMARK-GOLD` more gold from their troops and workers (`R-ECO-03`, `R-ECO-07`);
  - growth: their troops grow `B-LANDMARK-GROWTH` faster (`R-ECO-02`), never past the cap;
  - fortress: attackers pay `B-LANDMARK-FORTRESS` of the usual price for the holder's tiles within `B-LANDMARK-FORTRESS-RADIUS` of it; where a fort or a capital covers the same tile, only the strongest applies (`R-BUILD-03`, `R-CAP-02`);
  - trade: `B-LANDMARK-TRADE` more gold from each trade ship that pays them (`R-UNIT-03`);
  - shield: on the tick a missile of a player who is neither the holder nor their ally first flies within `B-LANDMARK-SHIELD-RADIUS` of it, it stops the missile at `B-LANDMARK-SHIELD-CHANCE`, drawn from the game's seed, after any air defense has tried (`R-BUILD-05`); every player is told, as for an air defense;
  - assault: each tile the holder's attacks and ships take costs them `B-LANDMARK-ASSAULT` less (`R-ATTACK-03`).
- **R-LAND-03** Every player is told when a landmark changes hands, who took it and from whom.

## 10. Fairness and determinism

- **R-FAIR-01** Every client simulates the same game from the same seed and the same ordered list of player orders. The server checks orders but never decides outcomes.
- **R-FAIR-02** Any random choice in a game is drawn from the game's seed, never from the device.
- **R-FAIR-03** A player's orders take effect at the start of the next turn after the server receives them, in the order the server received them.

## Changing these rules

Open an issue describing the problem in play and the proposed rule. When the change is agreed, the pull request updates this file, adds or updates tests that cite the rule identifiers, and notes the change in [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md). Identifiers are never reused: a removed rule keeps its number, marked as withdrawn.
